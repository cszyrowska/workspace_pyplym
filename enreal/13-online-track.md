## 1. What this adds

**Online-first courses and content join EnReal's offer** [F]: fully online, self-paced courses, in the format of AlgoExpert or LeetCode rather than their content. They are made once, kept up to date, and run without the founder's time beyond updates and improvement [F]. They sit **alongside** the monthly issue, which stays exactly as planned (D-68).

**Why [F]:** an online offer can start as soon as there is anything to sell, and can scale beyond what local work allows. *Recorded as a hypothesis to test, not a finding:* that online can bring more money than any local business (§5, point 4).

## 4. Two properties to compare models by

These describe each model; they are not a filter (D-68).

- **Member-scalable:** the founder's hours do not grow with the number of members.
- **Evergreen:** made once; it needs updates, not monthly production.

| Offer | Member-scalable | Evergreen | Founder hours [A] |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Monthly issue | Yes, mostly (questions and comments grow a little) | No | About 16–18 a month ([PLAN §3](../../work/plan.md)) |
| Online course with automatic checks | Yes | Yes | Build once (unmeasured), then updates |
| Online course marked by a person | No | Yes | Grows with every learner |
| Programme, online edition | No | No | Grows with every cohort |
| Programme, local | No | No | Grows with every cohort, plus the venue |

## 5. What the comparators show (research, 20 October)

| Comparator | Free | Paid | Price |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **AlgoExpert** | Little | Video explanations, a code editor with tests, seven products in one bundle | About $119 a year; access for one year [V, secondary; its own page not reached] |
| **Boot.dev** | **All content, to read and watch** | Checking of submissions, an AI tutor, progress, game mechanics | $33 a month or $399 a year [V, secondary] |
| **DeepLearning.AI** | Most of 60–107 short AI courses, for an email address | Pro, for some newer material | About $300 a year [V, secondary] |
| **LeetCode** | A large bank of problems, judged automatically | Premium: more problems and features | [K] |
| **AI Evals for Engineers & PMs** (Husain and Shankar, on Maven) | Free short lessons | A **live cohort** on evaluating AI systems | About $4,200 for the October 2026 cohort; 768 reviews listed [V, secondary; enrolment and revenue not published] |
| **AI-engineering interview guides** | Several free | Guides and kits | From $2.99 [V, secondary; few prices published] |

**What follows [A]:**
1. **The paid value sits in the practice-and-check loop and a clear result, not in the content.** Boot.dev is the cleanest case: everything can be read free; people pay for the checking.
2. **Short AI courses have a free floor,** from well-funded providers.
3. **AlgoExpert's material (algorithms) stays true for years; AI tooling changes within months.** A course here needs a durable core (how people learn, delegate, judge; how retrieval, agents and evaluation work) and labs that can be swapped as the tools change.
4. **Online scales delivery, not demand.** The audience, not the delivery, is the likely limit. To be tested: the claim in §1.

## 6. Candidate courses (none chosen yet)

| | Buyer | What they hold at the end | Who checks it | Founder (20 Oct) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| D | **Business leaders:** AI-Bench's method, self-paced | One AI change in their business, measured | A person, unless the check is designed otherwise | **Liked** |
| B | **Non-technical knowledge workers** (can reuse Issue #1) | A personal tutor used for a week, and a self-test | Self-report, or an AI marking a reflection (unproven) | **A candidate** |
| C | **Job seekers:** AI-engineering interview preparation | Practice answers scored against rubrics | Partly a machine | **Later** |
| A | **Practitioners:** an evaluation harness for their own AI feature | A harness that catches planted failures | A machine (tests on the code) | **Not valuable,** in the founder's view |

**A point to keep in view for D:** what leaders pay for in AI-Bench is the peer group and the accountability (D-15), which a self-paced course removes, and explaining AI to leaders already has a free floor (for example Andrew Ng's *AI for Everyone* on Coursera [K]). So a self-paced D would sell **the result, one measured change**, and stands or falls on how that result is checked. Untested ways that avoid founder hours: peer review between learners (needs enough people starting together); an AI reviewer against a rubric; a paid review by the founder, priced as consulting. **Cheap evidence:** ask in the AI-Bench discovery conversations (January–March, [00 §10](00-vision-foundation.md)) whether a leader would take it self-paced with a check, or only with a peer group.

## 7. What the build must consider

**A checklist, not decisions.** Each item is settled when the build makes it clearer (D-68). Starting hypotheses are marked.

**The offer**
- [ ] The buyer and the result they hold at the end (§6).
- [ ] How the result is checked: a machine, peers, an AI reviewer, or a person (paid). This decides whether the course really runs without the founder's time.
- [ ] Packaging and price. *Starting hypothesis (D-69, proposed):* each course sold separately, at a members' price; buying one gives a free membership of EnReal online; a bundle considered once about three courses have real sales (bundling later adds value, unbundling later reads as a price rise).
- [ ] How a course relates to the issue: grown from an issue's topic (for example Issue #1 for B), or standing alone.

**The content**
- [ ] A durable core with swappable labs (§5, point 3).
- [ ] An update routine: who reviews each course, how often, and what triggers a revision.
- [ ] Founder hours to build one course: **predict before the first build, then log** (D-68: a measure, not a gate).
- [ ] Cecy's part (research and design), within her capped hours ([PLAN §4](../../work/plan.md)).

**The platform** (the operational choices follow from here)
- [ ] Self-paced access with progress. ProDanX's curriculum plugin has modules with progress; paid access is not enforced anywhere yet ([12 §2](12-prodanx-requirements.md)) [V].
- [ ] One-off purchases as well as subscriptions; a members' price; access granted on payment and removed on refund.
- [ ] The checks themselves: code run in a sandbox, or text reviewed against a rubric. Probably the costliest part to build [A].
- [ ] Web first, because of Apple's rules on paid digital content (D-49; softened in C-39).
- [ ] A ready-made course tool first and ProDanX later, as with the issue's fallback kit (D-57), or ProDanX from the start.

**Money, law and tax** (to check with the accountant) [K]
- [ ] VAT: a fully automated course is usually an *electronically supplied service*, unlike live teaching. Sales to consumers in the EU can need VAT from the first sale (the non-Union OSS scheme). A merchant of record (a platform that sells in its own name) can carry this.
- [ ] Consumer rights: the 14-day cancellation right for digital content ends only if the buyer asks for immediate access and acknowledges losing it. A published refund policy.
- [ ] UK subscription rules (Digital Markets, Competition and Consumers Act 2024) if a bundle is sold as a subscription: commencement and duties to check.
- [ ] Any platform fee goes into the [cost register](../../company/money/costs.md) before it is paid.

**People and boundaries**
- [ ] Learner support: who answers. Customer contact is outside Kamila's role (D-24) unless she agrees.
- [ ] The contract: generic content only, nothing from the client's systems, no marketing to the end client ([00 §9](00-vision-foundation.md)).
- [ ] Privacy: learner records stay in the course tool, never in this repository ([privacy.md](../../company/handbook/privacy.md)).
- [ ] Claims in the future tense until a course exists ([00 §8](00-vision-foundation.md)).

**Selling**
- [ ] Where buyers come from with about £0 for marketing (D-03): the issue's free members, PyPlym, LinkedIn, the Kaggle work, marketplaces.
- [ ] The claim in §1: the cheapest test that could prove it wrong.
- [ ] What to count: who saw it, who bought, who finished, who asked for a refund (as in [10 §9](10-online-edition-options.md)).
